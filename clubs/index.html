How a team turns into an internship Our advice

1
First weeks of fall
Shop around

Go to the club fair and two or three info sessions. Most teams recruit right at the start of the year.

2
By October
Pick one and go deep

One team where you own real work beats five where you are a name on a list.

3
First semester
Own one part

A bracket, a battery mount, a pitch, a script. Something you can point at and say "I built that."

4
Before the career fair
Write it like a job

Put the team under Experience: what you built, what tools, what number changed. Bring photos.

5
At the fair and after
Talk about the part

Recruiters ask "tell me about a project." Companies that back teams know them. Next year, lead a sub-team.

The honest part: a team doesn't hand you an internship. It gives you real work to talk about, people a year or two ahead who already interned, and sometimes companies that sponsor the team and come looking.

How to pick one Our advice

Build, analyze, or sell?

Design teams build things. Investment funds and consulting clubs analyze. Most big teams also need people for sponsors, marketing and money.

Does it point at jobs you want?

Each card below links to live openings in the areas that team trains you for. Look before you join.

Can a first-year do real work?

Ask at the info session: "What did your newest members build last year?" Vague answer, keep looking.

How much time is it really?

Ask current members, not the flyer. Competition months are heavy. Pick what fits next to your classes.

Any major welcome?

Often, yes. Big teams need people for sponsors, marketing and money, and many funds and clubs take every major. Check "Who joins" on each card.
