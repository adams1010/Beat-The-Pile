Safe job search

Fake job offers are common, and students get targeted a lot. Here's how to spot them, what we do on our side, and what to do if something feels off. Last updated November 21, 2026.

What Beat the Pile will never do

  • Charge you anything. Students never pay to use Beat the Pile or to apply to a job.
  • Text you or message you on social media with a job offer.
  • Ask for your Social Security number, bank details, or a payment to "hold" a job.
  • Offer you a job. We don't hire for companies. We point you to each company's own job board, and you apply there.

What we do on our side

  • Jobs only from the company's own board. A company only shows up once our robot proves the job board is really theirs. We don't take postings from job aggregators or reposters.
  • Dead links come down. We re-check postings twice a day and hide ones that closed.
  • Employers are checked. A company page, the "Recruiter" badge and the ability to message students all require a confirmed work email at the company's own website domain, then a review by us.
  • You choose who can reach you. Only verified employers can message students, and only students who turned on "Let employers find me". You can block or report anyone from a chat.

Red flags

  • The email doesn't match the company. A real recruiter usually writes from the company's own domain (name@company.com), not Gmail, Yahoo or a look-alike such as company-careers.net.
  • It's a different job than the one you applied for, or you're "hired" without ever talking to a person by video or in person.
  • Money moves first. They send you a check and ask you to send some back, want you to buy equipment or gift cards, or ask you to receive and forward payments. These are scams.
  • Too good to be true. High pay, no experience, fully remote, start tomorrow, and pressure to decide right now.
  • They want sensitive details early. Your Social Security number, bank account or ID photos before a real offer letter.

Our advice. When in doubt, go to the company's own website, find the job there, and contact them through the details on that site.

If something feels off

  • On Beat the Pile: use Report in the chat or on the profile, or the Report a problem button on any page. You can also email support@beatthepile.com. We look at every report.
  • Already sent money or personal details? Contact your bank right away, then report it to the Federal Trade Commission at reportfraud.ftc.gov and, for online fraud, the FBI at ic3.gov. If your Social Security number was shared, see identitytheft.gov.
  • At school: your career center can tell you whether an employer is known to them.

Keep your account safe

  • Sign in only at beatthepile.com. Your sign-in code is for you alone: nobody from Beat the Pile will ever ask you to tell it to them.
  • Keep your resume link private unless you're handing it out at a fair. You can turn it off or get a new link any time in your profile.
  • Your phone number and email only show to signed-in members, and only if you leave "Show my contact info on my profile" on.
